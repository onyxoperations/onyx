The Architect

Faraz Shahzad is the Founder & CEO of Onyx HRMS — and the architect who designed and engineered the platform from a blank page.

His premise was simple. The software running a company's most valuable asset shouldn't make HR work harder. It should disappear into the work, so teams can focus on building, not bookkeeping. So he didn't stitch together attendance, payroll and people tools. He architected a single connected system where each part feeds the next — natively, with no sync, no duplicates, no drift.

From the data model to the geo-fenced attendance engine to the payroll pipeline, every layer was drawn to carry ten employees and five hundred on the same foundation.

That architecture is why Onyx scales without re-platforming, why a successful candidate becomes an employee in a single step, and why payroll calculates itself from attendance without a human in the middle. It's also why the operator's data stays inside their own workspace — privacy by architecture, not a setting toggled on after.

What he built

The architecture
behind Onyx.

Six pillars Faraz designed and engineered — each one a system, together one platform.

01

Connected data model

A single source of truth where employee records, departments, reporting lines and documents live as one graph — no sync, no duplicates, no drift.

02

Geo-fenced biometrics

Every employee's smartphone becomes a check-in terminal — geo-fenced, timestamped, logged in real time, with no biometric hardware required.

03

Automated payroll engine

Attendance flows straight into payroll. Adjustments, deductions and net pay calculate themselves, and salary slips generate in one click.

04

Employee lifecycle

From recruitment pipeline to onboarding to offboarding — the full journey, so a successful candidate becomes a profile in a single step.

05

Role-based access

Permissions engineered at every layer. People see only what they need to, sensitive records stay restricted, every action stays traceable.

06

Document & template engine

HR letters, salary slips and documents generate consistently from live employee data — with a template editor that puts control back in the operator's hands.

Operating principles

How he builds.

The convictions that shape every decision Faraz makes as architect of Onyx.

01

Build for real operations

Software that looks beautiful in a demo but breaks on a real shift roster is useless. Onyx is built against the messy reality of how teams actually run.

02

One system, not many

Disconnected tools create disconnected teams. Faraz architected one connected workspace so data never has to be copied, exported or reconciled.

03

Privacy by architecture

People data is sacred. Onyx runs inside the operator's own drive with role-based access applied across every record, not bolted on after.

04

Scale without re-platforming

The architecture was drawn to carry ten employees and five hundred on the same foundation — so growth never means starting over.
